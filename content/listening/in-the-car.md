Both work through the phone app, so whatever is downloaded on your phone plays in the car with no signal.

## Android Auto

**Browse.** Two tabs:

- **Continue listening**: up to eight books in progress, each with its author and the time left. The tab is hidden when nothing is in progress.
- **Library**: **Downloaded**, **By series** and **By author**.

**Ask for it.** Voice search understands three kinds of request: resuming ("continue my audiobook"), moving through a series ("next book"), and a title or author by name.

**Now playing.** Alongside play and pause you get skip back and skip forward, using the [intervals you chose](/listening/player/#skipping), and a **Speed** button that steps through the presets.

**Not signed in?** The car shows "Sign in to ListenUp on your phone." Sign in on the phone and it fills in. If your session has only lapsed, you can still browse and play what's on the phone.

## Apple CarPlay

**Browse.** Two tabs:

- **Continue listening**: eight books in progress, with author and time left.
- **Library**: your books, in the same order as on your phone.

Tap a book and it plays.

**Now playing.** A playback-speed button, previous and next chapter, and a **Chapters** button that lists the book's chapters so you can jump straight to one.

## How they differ

| | Android Auto | CarPlay |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Browse by series and author | ✅ | Books only |
| Search in the car | ✅ voice | Through Siri |

## Chapters, not hours

On both, the progress bar and the time shown are for the **current chapter**, and previous and next move by chapter. Previous goes to the start of the chapter you're in first. It's the same rule as the [player](/listening/player/) on your phone, and it keeps a glance at the dash meaningful: "8 minutes left" is something you can act on, and "14 hours left" isn't.
